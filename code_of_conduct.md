# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

Everyone who takes part in Snapdot's issue tracker, discussions, and documentation — users, contributors, and maintainers — pledges to make this a welcoming, respectful place to be. We want people of every background, skill level, and perspective to feel at home here.

This applies regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Use welcoming and inclusive language.
- Be respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences.
- Give and receive constructive feedback with care.
- Focus on what is best for the community and the project.
- Show empathy toward other community members.
- Assume good faith. Most misunderstandings in writing are just that — misunderstandings.

## What we will not accept

- Insulting, derogatory, or dismissive comments. Personal attacks of any kind.
- Public or private harassment of any community member.
- Sharing someone else's private information — physical address, email, workplace, phone number — without their explicit permission.
- Unwanted sexual attention or imagery, in any context.
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, or following, online or offline.
- Any other behavior that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

Disagreement is fine. Disagreement is often how the project improves. The line is between "I think this design is wrong, and here is why" and anything that attacks the person on the other side of the keyboard.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within every space the project operates in — the issue tracker, pull request discussions on the documentation files, the Discussions tab, and any community spaces linked from the repository. It also applies when a community member is representing the project in public, for example at a conference, in a social-media post that references the project, or in a blog post about Snapdot.

## Reporting

If you see or experience behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, please report it to the maintainers at the address listed in `SECURITY.md`. Reports can be made in any language the maintainers can read.

A report should include:

- What happened, in your own words.
- Where it happened — a link to the issue, discussion, or post is ideal.
- When it happened, if the content is time-sensitive.
- Whether you have taken any action already, such as blocking or muting.
- What outcome you would like, if you have one in mind. "I would like them warned", "I would prefer not to be contacted by this person again", or "I just want this noted" are all valid.

Maintainers will acknowledge the report within three business days. All reports are handled with discretion. The person being reported will not be told who filed the report unless the reporter explicitly agrees, and even then only when it is necessary to resolve the situation.

## Enforcement

The maintainers follow a four-step ladder, picking the step that fits the severity of the behavior and the pattern of the person involved:

1. **A private note.** A one-on-one message from a maintainer explaining what was off and why. Most situations end here. No public record is kept.
2. **A warning.** A formal note that further incidents will lead to a temporary ban. The warned person is asked to avoid interaction with the reporter — including through friends or on other platforms — for a set period.
3. **A temporary ban.** Removal of posting rights across all project spaces for a defined period, usually between seven and ninety days. Violating the terms of the ban extends it or escalates to the next step.
4. **A permanent ban.** For patterns of harassment, aggression, or escalations after a ban. Permanent across every project space, with no re-application process.

The maintainers may skip steps for severe single incidents — threats of violence, doxing, sexual harassment of a minor, and so on — and move directly to step three or four. These cases are rare and are documented internally with the reasoning.

## Appeals

If you believe an enforcement decision was wrong, you may appeal in writing to the maintainers within thirty days. Appeals are reviewed by a maintainer who was not involved in the original decision. The outcome of an appeal is final.
